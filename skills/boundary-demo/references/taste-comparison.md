# Taste comparison mode

Use this mode only when interaction, information hierarchy, or visual
representation is the uncertain decision.

## Hold the experiment constant

Compare two or three variants using the same:

- user and task;
- content and representative cases;
- viewport and state;
- comparison axis;
- implementation fidelity.

Variants must disagree about structure, hierarchy, or a signature interaction.
A palette or typography swap is not a meaningful branch.

Before viewing results, record a prediction and its reason. After trying the
same cases, record:

```text
Comparison axis
Prediction before
Human choice after
Why each rejected variant lost
Which case changed or confirmed the judgment
Prediction for the next similar situation
```

Do not replace pairwise judgment with a self-authored absolute score. “More
polished”, “premium”, and “cleaner” are not evidence unless tied to a task,
observable behavior, or remembered distinction.

Use existing design-direction skills only when available and relevant. They may
generate or preflight variants, but they may not decide the user's taste. Use
browser screenshots only when actual rendering is part of the comparison.

Run normal, edge, and failure cases for the selected taste task itself. Keep
Taste as the single mode; do not require a separate Boundary-mode run. The
purpose is to train predictive judgment, not to decorate an untested happy
path.
